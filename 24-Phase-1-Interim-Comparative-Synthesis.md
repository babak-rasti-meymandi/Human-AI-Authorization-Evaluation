# Phase 1 Interim Comparative Synthesis — BAES, NIST AI RMF 1.0, ISO/IEC 42001:2023

**Date:** 2026-11-02  
**Status:** Interim investigator synthesis; not frozen; independent second-coder verification pending.  
**Research phase:** Phase 1 — documentary reconstruction and evidence sufficiency.  
**Criterion basis:** Recovered Historical C1–C20 only.  
**Not a finding of:** conformity, certification, validity, novelty, causal effect, effectiveness, or framework superiority.

## 1. Purpose and boundary

This document consolidates the initial Phase 1 investigator-side findings across three distinct evidence domains:

1. **Axis A — Framework Text Coverage (T0–T3 / UL):** what the examined public text of each framework explicitly or indirectly addresses.
2. **Axis B — Event Evidence Sufficiency (R0–R3):** what the examined synthetic S01-A and S01-B evidence bundles support for each historical criterion.
3. **Axis C — Residual Ambiguity:** what remains unknown, what alternative interpretations remain possible, why the uncertainty matters, and what evidence would resolve it.

These axes are not combined. No composite score, weighted total, or overall framework ranking is calculated.

## 2. Source and method boundary

### BAES

The BAES mapping uses only the public repository `babak-rasti-meymandi/BAES-Public`, primarily `01_BAES_Introduction.md`, with public academic, technology, and enterprise context pages as supporting material. No BAES-SECRET or private research content was accessed or reproduced.

The public BAES record describes the standard as under development. Its proposed evaluation dimensions are explicitly unvalidated. The mapping therefore records public conceptual text coverage only.

### NIST

The NIST mapping uses *Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)*, NIST AI 100-1 (2023), especially the public Core in §§5.1–5.4. The NIST Core presents GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, and MANAGE as risk-management functions and states that its actions are not a checklist or necessarily ordered steps.

### ISO

The ISO/IEC 42001:2023 mapping is **UL — unreviewed/access-limited** for every historical criterion. The official publication records establish the standard's identity, edition, date, and organizational AI management-system scope, but the complete final-edition clause text was not examined. No clause-level mapping is inferred from an FDIS-era crosswalk or third-party reproduction.

### Scenario evidence

S01-A and S01-B are constructed research stimuli, not historical or production events. The proposed code is not attributed to a named AI model. Neither packet establishes AI observation/reasoning provenance or actual execution.

## 3. Axis A — Initial public text coverage

| Framework | Initial coverage pattern | Main direct areas | Main limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| BAES public formulation | T3: C3, C4, C11, C18; T2: most other criteria; T1: C10 | Explicit conceptual definitions for delegation, authority scope, human decision, and traceability | Does not prescribe complete event-level record schemas, implementation procedures, or validated performance |
| NIST AI RMF 1.0 Core | T2 for several related governance, scope, oversight, documentation, decision, and evaluation topics; T1 for indirect topics; T0 for C7; no T3 | Organizational roles, human-AI oversight, system context/scope, decision-support documentation, evaluation, monitoring, and risk/change management | The reviewed Core does not expressly establish the complete task-level authorization and provenance chain in the historical criteria |
| ISO/IEC 42001:2023 | UL for C1–C20 | Officially established organizational AIMS scope | Clause-level coverage not assessed from sufficient authorized final-edition text |

### Interpretation

The public BAES text is directly aligned in subject matter with several historical criteria concerning delegation, authority scope, human decision, and traceability. NIST AI RMF 1.0 addresses important adjacent organizational and AI-system risk-management topics. These sources have different scopes and purposes; a greater number of direct conceptual matches in this particular criterion set is not, by itself, evidence of better overall quality or superiority.

T3 for BAES means that a public definition/minimum distinguishing condition closely matches the criterion's central concept. It does not mean BAES supplies a validated implementation mechanism or proves that a real system complies with the concept.

NIST T0 for C7 is bounded to the reviewed AI RMF 1.0 Core. It is not a finding that no NIST publication, Playbook suggestion, or other NIST resource addresses related activity distinctions.

ISO UL is an access/status limitation, not a negative coverage result.

## 4. Axis B — S01 evidence sufficiency

### S01-A

- **R3:** C1, C2, C11, C12, C13, C15, C16.
- **R2:** C7, C9, C10, C17, C18, C19, C20.
- **R0:** C3, C4, C5, C6, C8, C14.

S01-A's synthetic record supports a bounded human decision, decision scope, authorization change, and acceptance of the semantic deviation. It does not establish AI-agent delegation, AI provenance, or execution. Its H1 amendment does not establish the authorization state at an unknown execution time.

### S01-B

- **R3:** C1, C2, C15.
- **R2:** C7, C9, C10, C11, C18, C20.
- **R1:** C12, C16.
- **R0:** C3, C4, C5, C6, C8, C13, C14, C17, C19.

S01-B records PR approval but does not identify the approver or establish the scope of approval, informed acceptance of the deviation, authorization amendment, or execution. Inferring acceptance from PR approval would exceed the supplied evidence.

### Critical distinction

Both variants directly evidence the semantic deviation (C15 R3), while neither evidences actual AI execution provenance (C8 R0) or task execution (C14 R0). This is not contradictory: identifying a proposed semantic change is a different claim from proving that an AI system executed it.

## 5. Axis C — Principal residual ambiguities

The consolidated AMB-01–AMB-09 register in the Phase 1 audit remains applicable. Its principal groups are:

1. AI system identity and provenance;
2. agent delegation and its scope;
3. human review content and materials inspected;
4. human decision-maker identity and decision scope;
5. authorization amendment and acceptance;
6. actual execution and resulting outcome;
7. authorization state at execution time, including amendments/revocations;
8. event chronology and end-to-end traceability; and
9. provenance/version history of the recovered historical criteria.

The public S01 packet cannot resolve these gaps. R0 means no relevant evidence for the specific claim in the examined packet; it does not prove that the event did not occur elsewhere.

## 6. What the present evidence does and does not support

### Supported at this stage

- The recovered historical C1–C20 set has been coded at investigator level for S01-A and S01-B.
- An initial BAES public-text mapping and NIST AI RMF 1.0 Core mapping have been recorded separately.
- ISO/IEC 42001:2023 clause-level mapping has been correctly held at UL rather than guessed.
- The S01 evidence contrast is reconstructable at a bounded synthetic level: A contains explicit scenario authorization/amendment; B contains PR approval without specific authorization established by the packet.
- The technical deviation can be identified independently of whether it was accepted or executed.

### Not established

- That BAES is novel or unique.
- That BAES improves reviewer performance, reduces errors, or produces better decisions.
- That BAES is superior to NIST AI RMF 1.0 or ISO/IEC 42001:2023.
- That the public BAES concepts have been implemented or validated in an operational system.
- That any named AI system generated or executed the S01 code.
- That any human reviewer or independent second coder has assessed the packets.
- That ISO/IEC 42001 lacks coverage of any historical criterion.

## 8. Overall interim conclusion

The current evidence supports a limited statement: **the public BAES formulation explicitly defines several conceptual distinctions that are central to the recovered historical criteria, while NIST AI RMF 1.0 addresses related organizational and AI-system risk-management outcomes.** The current public-source boundary does not permit a criterion-level conclusion about ISO/IEC 42001:2023.

The S01 packets demonstrate a synthetic contrast in the documentation of human authorization, not a real AI execution event and not a comparative test of framework effectiveness. The findings do not establish BAES added value or superiority. Negative, mixed, and unresolved outcomes remain valid possible conclusions as the research proceeds.

No composite score or ranking is produced.
