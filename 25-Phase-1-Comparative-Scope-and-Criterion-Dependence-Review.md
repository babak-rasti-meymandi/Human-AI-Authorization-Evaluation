# Phase 1 — Comparative Scope and Criterion-Dependence Review

**Date:** 2026-11-02  
**Status:** Initial investigator-side methodological review; not independent validation.  
**Research phase:** Phase 1 — documentary reconstruction and evidence sufficiency.  
**Applies to:** Axis A framework-text coverage, Axis B S01 event-evidence sufficiency, Axis C residual ambiguity, and the interim synthesis.  
**Criterion basis:** Recovered Historical C1–C20 only.

## 1. Purpose

This review makes explicit the limits on interpreting the initial three-source comparison. It does not change any T-level, R-level, criterion wording, or ambiguity status. Its purpose is to prevent a criterion-conditioned, source-limited comparison from being misreported as a comprehensive comparison of entire frameworks.

## 2. Researcher’s interim milestone statement

The researcher identified the following as an important achievement of the current work:

> «به نظرم یک دستاورد مهم به دست آورده‌ایم: اکنون می‌توانیم با سند و ارجاع نشان دهیم که BAES در برخی مفاهیم، تعریف‌های عمومی صریحی دارد؛ NIST در زمینه‌های مدیریت ریسک و نظارت سازمانی پوشش مرتبطی دارد؛ و درباره ISO هنوز اطلاعات کافی برای قضاوت بندبه‌بند نداریم.»

Working English rendering:

> “I think we have achieved something important: we can now show, with documentary evidence and references, that BAES has explicit public definitions for some concepts; NIST has related coverage in risk management and organizational oversight; and we still do not have enough information to judge ISO clause by clause.”

This is an appropriate interim milestone statement when read within the source and criterion boundaries below. It is not a claim of framework superiority, novelty, implementation, effectiveness, or ISO non-coverage.

## 3. The comparison is criterion-conditioned, not comprehensive

The Axis A mapping asks how the examined public framework texts relate to the recovered historical C1–C20 evaluation questions. Those questions focus on human intent, delegation, authority, AI activity, decisions, authorization, execution, traceability, and reconstruction.

The recovered criteria are not normative requirements of BAES, NIST AI RMF 1.0, or ISO/IEC 42001:2023. Their original date, version, and storage provenance remain unverified; the available source is a researcher-supplied historical hand-over. The criterion set must therefore be treated as a recovered evaluation instrument with unresolved provenance, not as an independently established neutral or exhaustive sampling frame for all three frameworks.

Consequences:

1. A T-level describes the relationship between a particular examined source and a particular recovered criterion.
2. A higher number of direct matches within this criterion set does not establish broader framework quality, completeness, maturity, or superiority.
3. The mapping cannot establish that the criteria represent the full scope or priorities of any framework.
4. The findings should not be generalized to framework topics outside C1–C20.
5. No criterion may be silently reworded or replaced to improve apparent alignment. Any future alternative criterion set must remain separately versioned and justified.

## 4. Source coverage is asymmetric

The current three-source pass does not examine equivalent bodies of material:

| Source | Material examined | What the current pass can say | What it cannot say |
|---|---|---|---|
| BAES | Public BAES repository; primary introduction plus public academic, technology, and enterprise context pages | Whether the examined public formulation explicitly or indirectly defines concepts relevant to C1–C20 | Whether private material adds further content; whether concepts are implemented, validated, or operationally complete |
| NIST | NIST AI RMF 1.0 public Core, especially GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, and MANAGE subcategories | Whether the reviewed Core contains related organizational and AI-system risk-management outcomes | Whether every NIST publication, Playbook resource, profile, or implementation guide addresses a related topic |
| ISO/IEC 42001:2023 | Official public bibliographic/product information; insufficient final-edition clause text for criterion-level review | The identity, edition, publication record, and broad AIMS scope of the standard | Any positive or negative clause-level coverage conclusion for C1–C20 |

This asymmetry is a known limitation, not a reason to fill gaps by inference. In particular:

- NIST T0 for C7 is bounded to the reviewed AI RMF 1.0 Core. It is not a claim that no NIST resource addresses related distinctions.
- ISO UL means unreviewed/access-limited. It is neither T0 nor evidence of absence.
- BAES T3 records explicit public conceptual definitions/minimum distinguishing conditions for C3, C4, C11, and C18. It does not mean a validated implementation mechanism or complete event-record schema is present.

## 5. Interpretation of the current milestone

The current evidence supports this bounded interpretation:

- The examined public BAES formulation contains explicit definitions and conceptual boundaries that closely correspond to several of the recovered criteria, especially delegated authority, authority scope, human decision, and traceability.
- The reviewed NIST AI RMF 1.0 Core contains related organizational risk-management, governance, human-oversight, documentation, evaluation, and lifecycle concepts, but is not being treated as a task-level authorization ledger.
- The current public-source boundary does not support criterion-by-criterion conclusions about ISO/IEC 42001:2023.
- The synthetic S01-A/S01-B evidence comparison is a separate Axis B exercise. It does not establish implementation or effectiveness of any framework.

The achievement is therefore one of **documented conceptual differentiation and bounded comparative visibility**. It is not yet evidence that BAES adds practical value, improves decisions, or outperforms an existing framework.

## 6. Cross-axis safeguards

The following boundaries remain mandatory:

1. Axis A T-levels assess examined framework text; Axis B R-levels assess evidence in a specific synthetic S01 packet; Axis C records residual ambiguity.
2. T-levels do not determine R-levels, and R-levels do not validate framework text.
3. The existence of a BAES concept does not prove its implementation in S01.
4. The presence of an S01 artifact does not prove that a framework requires that artifact.
5. Investigator-side C20 coding is not an observed independent reviewer result.
6. No T/R/ambiguity values are averaged, weighted, ranked, or converted into a composite score.
7. The current interim synthesis remains non-frozen and subject to independent second-coder verification.

## 7. Required next validation work

Priority order:

1. **Independent second-coder review:** independently code the recovered historical C1–C20 criteria and verify the Axis A and Axis B judgments, with disagreements retained and resolved transparently rather than by forced consensus.
2. **Source-scope verification:** preserve exact source editions, sections, and examined boundaries. If NIST resources beyond the Core are added, record them as a separately scoped extension rather than silently changing the present mapping.
3. **ISO access gate:** obtain sufficient authorized access to the final ISO/IEC 42001:2023 edition before attempting clause-level coding. Until then, retain UL for C1–C20.
4. **Criterion provenance recovery:** attempt to recover the original hand-over file identity, date/version, and source location. If unavailable, retain the provenance limitation.
5. **Phase 2 gate:** do not treat investigator-side reconstruction as reviewer performance. Any human study requires the held-out scenario, controlled reviewer access, frozen materials, and separate reporting.

If no independent coder is available, retain all findings as investigator-side exploratory results. Do not describe internal self-review as independent verification.

## 8. Conclusion

The current Phase 1 milestone is meaningful because the examined public BAES text, NIST AI RMF 1.0 Core, and the available ISO publication information can now be described with explicit references and clear limits. The comparison shows related but non-identical conceptual coverage in the examined BAES and NIST materials, while ISO clause-level coverage remains unknown under the present access boundary.

This conclusion is intentionally narrower than a framework ranking. It preserves the possibility of positive, negative, mixed, or inconclusive findings after independent coding and any properly authorized source expansion.

No T-level, R-level, criterion wording, or Axis C ambiguity rating is changed by this review.
