## Coding status definitions

Use the project's evidence labels consistently:

- **Supported:** the available packet evidence affirmatively supports the criterion.
- **Contradicted:** the available packet evidence affirmatively conflicts with the criterion.
- **Not established:** the packet does not provide enough evidence to decide; this does not mean the event did not occur.
- **Ambiguous:** the evidence supports materially different interpretations that cannot be resolved from the packet.
- **Partial:** use only as a descriptive interim finding where some, but not all, aspects of a criterion are evidenced; state exactly what is and is not supported. Do not treat Partial as a formal historical criterion label.
- **Not assessed:** coding has not yet been performed or is deliberately deferred.
- **Not applicable:** use only with a case-specific written rationale.

The recovered historical criteria do not themselves define a complete coding-status taxonomy. These working labels are operational recording conventions for this Phase 1 worksheet, not additions to or amendments of the historical criterion wording.

Do not infer an event from a planned artifact. Do not treat the scenario's intended lesson as evidence. Keep observed facts separate from investigator interpretation. Each material conclusion must cite an artifact and the relevant detail. If no evidence supports a claim, state what is missing instead of inventing a reference.

## Quality checks before closing the worksheet

- [ ] All cited artifact IDs exist in the applicable packet or investigator manifest.
- [ ] Every criterion status has a rationale.
- [ ] Every material claim is grounded in a supplied artifact.
- [ ] “Not established” is not reported as “did not happen.”
- [ ] Synthetic scenario premises are not presented as real or independently authenticated events.
- [ ] AI observation, reasoning, proposal, and execution are not attributed without provenance.
- [ ] Human review, decision, authorization, and execution remain distinct.
- [ ] Technical correctness is not treated as proof of authorization.
- [ ] Investigator-only evidence is identified and not misrepresented as reviewer-visible evidence.
- [ ] No composite score is calculated.
- [ ] Any change to historical criterion wording or coding rules is separately logged and versioned.

## Separation from Proposed v0.1 and the reviewer experiment

This worksheet uses only the recovered historical criterion names and principal questions. It does not substitute the later Proposed C1–C20 v0.1 wording. The two sets must remain separate and must not be merged or silently cross-mapped.

The existing [Proposed v0.1 coding worksheet](13-Criterion-Coding-Worksheet.md) remains associated with [Proposed Evaluation Criteria C1–C20, draft v0.1](12-Proposed-Criteria-C1-C20.md). The existing [NIST crosswalk](11-Criterion-Crosswalk-Template.md) also remains explicitly based on Proposed v0.1; it is not a crosswalk for the recovered historical criteria.

This worksheet is for investigator-side Phase 1 documentary coding. It does not replace the common reviewer recording sheet and must not be silently added to a reviewer condition. Any decision to expose C1–C20 prompts to reviewers would change the intervention and must be decided and documented before formal evaluation.

No reviewer data are implied by a completed worksheet. The historical source's original date/version remains unverified, and this worksheet is an operational aid rather than a validated instrument.
